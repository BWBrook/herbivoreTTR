## Question and comparisons

Do the undisturbed exits persist under the existing informed and cyclic
comparators, and how does the encounter coefficient interact with policy in
the finite disturbance experiment?

1. Primary missing controls: informed and cyclic policies in clustered and
   fragmented layouts, unchanged growth, near water, at the existing .6 m²/h
   coefficient. Compare with the experienced policy from the same day-60 state.
2. Sensitivity: coefficients .3, .6, 1.5, 3 and 5 m²/h. For each coefficient,
   run three layouts × two water settings × experienced/informed policies
   under the 84-day growth reduction (12 branches), plus clustered/fragmented
   layouts × experienced/informed/cyclic policies under unchanged growth and
   near water (six branches). This is 90 planned post-baseline branches.

The coefficient applies from day 1. Each coefficient/layout has one experienced
physical-access baseline through day 60; policies branch from its identical
complete state on day 61. Thus policy comparisons have matched histories;
coefficient comparisons include altered histories. The policy contrast is a
day-61 intervention, not an all-life comparison of behavioural traits.
An exit before baseline completion is reported explicitly and its proposed
post-baseline contrasts are not evaluable. It is not attributed to a policy
which has not yet been applied.

All other animal, plant, residual, geometry, memory, water, travel and timing
parameters retain 062. Horizon is day 228; forcing is days 61–144. No new
coefficient points, policy changes, recovery rescue or duration search will be
added after viewing results. The grid is deliberately exploratory, not an
empirical plausible range or a probability distribution. It includes the
previous coefficient and Fable's suggested approximately 1.5 m²/h boundary.

## Predictions and interpretation

Fable predicts all four primary controls complete, and high search permits
informed sustained runs to complete while experienced runs remain confined
and exit. These are hypotheses to challenge, not acceptance criteria. Changing
encounter rates can change both the set of visited stations and plant history;
neither monotonicity nor persistent four-block confinement is assumed.

Completion is positive reserve through a finite horizon, not recovery,
equilibrium, biological survival or optimality. Failure remains a model-domain
exit. Ratios of supply, access, travel and reserve/recovery times describe each
contract; they do not turn synthetic parameters into an empirical organism.
The analytical one-block benchmark must declare its support area and feeding
assumptions separately from the spatial animal and its appetite feedback.
